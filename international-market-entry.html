International Market-Entry Advisory

Go-to-market help for the move across a border, not just across a budget line.

Your product can work in a new market. Your GTM playbook from the last one usually can't.

I've built go-to-market motions across ANZ, APAC, and the Middle East myself, not advised on them from a slide. At Brocade Communications, I ran the ANZ patch before the scope widened to all of APAC, carrying named accounts like Nestlé Australia, ANZ Bank, and Barclays, and was recognised at the company's global sales kickoff in San Francisco for more than doubling my first quarter's target.

This service is for two kinds of businesses: Indian companies taking their product abroad for the first time, and global or regional companies entering India, or APAC, ANZ, and the Middle East more broadly, without anyone on the ground who's actually run go-to-market in that market before.

Most international expansion fails quietly, not loudly. The product ships, the local website goes live, a few leads trickle in, and eighteen months later nobody can explain why it never really took off. It's rarely one dramatic mistake. It's usually a string of basic things nobody flagged before launch.

How This Works

Here's What This Actually Looks Like

  • Market and segment prioritisation, so the first push lands somewhere winnable, not just somewhere large.
  • Local positioning and pricing, adjusted for how the new market actually buys, not a straight currency conversion of the home-market deck.
  • Partner and channel structure: who you actually need on the ground, and what they need from you to sell.
  • The basic groundwork most companies skip before launch, trademark registration among them, flagged early enough to fix cheaply instead of expensively.

That APAC/ANZ scope at Brocade is also where I learned the lesson this service is built around: a go-to-market motion built for one market rarely survives a straight copy-paste into another. Buying committees, channel economics, and even what counts as a credible reference customer change by region, and the companies that assume otherwise usually find out at the worst possible time, mid-launch, not before it.

This is go-to-market advisory, not legal or tax advisory. I'll flag the basics worth getting in front of your own lawyer or company secretary early, trademark registration is the one that burns the most companies unnecessarily, see the note below, but the actual filing stays with your own counsel.

Good fit if:

you're planning, or already mid-way through, an international launch and want someone who has actually run GTM in that market, pressure-testing the plan before more spend goes in.

Not a fit if:

you already have a seasoned local GTM lead in place and just need execution hands. That's closer to my GTM Consulting or Fractional / Interim CMO services instead.

Frequently Asked

FAQs: International Market-Entry Advisory

What counts as international market entry here?
Either direction: Indian companies expanding into a new country, and companies entering India or the wider APAC, ANZ, or Middle East region. Same underlying work either way, prioritising a market, positioning for it, and building the channel to sell into it.
Do you handle the legal and regulatory side of entering a new market?
No, that stays with your own lawyers and company secretary. I flag what to raise with them early, trademark protection and basic data/compliance requirements among them, so it doesn't get missed, but the filing itself isn't mine to do.
How is this different from your GTM Consulting service?
GTM Consulting fixes something that's underperforming in a market you already operate in. This is specifically about the decisions that only come up once, at the start of a new market: which segment to enter first, how to price and position locally, and what channel structure to build.
What markets have you actually worked in?
ANZ and APAC directly, through Brocade Communications, carrying named accounts like Nestlé Australia, ANZ Bank, and Barclays. India, across every stage of my career since.
We haven't launched yet, is it too early to bring you in?
That's actually the ideal time. The cheapest point to catch a wrong assumption is before launch, not six months after, once spend and reputation are already attached to it.
What do I actually receive?
A written market-entry assessment covering segment prioritisation, local positioning, and channel structure, plus direct conversations with your team as needed through the engagement.

Worth Knowing

“A survey of 500 brands found half had already had their trademark pirated in a new market, an average of four times each, before they'd even properly entered it.”

Trademark protection doesn't travel with you. It has to be filed fresh in every market you enter, and the businesses that wait until after launch to do it are usually the ones that find someone else already filed first.

Source: survey by IP specialist Joe Simone's firm, reported by Marketplace / American Public Media, 2019
