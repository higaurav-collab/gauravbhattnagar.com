Frequently Asked

FAQs: Independent Director / Board Seats

What's the difference between being on the IICA databank and holding a board seat?
The databank confirms eligibility: that I've cleared the required exam and can legally be appointed as an independent director. It isn't an appointment itself. I'm eligible and available now; the seat is what I'm building next.
What are an independent director's legal responsibilities?
Independent directors carry fiduciary and oversight duties under the Companies Act, 2013 and, for listed companies, SEBI's LODR regulations, including duties around related-party transactions, risk oversight, and financial reporting integrity. Liability under Section 149(12) generally attaches only to acts that occurred with the director's knowledge or consent, but the oversight duty itself is real and taken seriously.
How many boards can an independent director serve on at once?
Section 165 of the Companies Act caps total directorships, with a tighter limit specifically for independent directorships at listed companies. Since I'm not yet serving on any board, that isn't a constraint here. I have full bandwidth.
What should a company expect to pay a first-time independent director?
Remuneration is typically a sitting fee per meeting plus, where permitted, a commission tied to company profits. The range depends heavily on company size and stage. I'd rather work out the right structure for your company on a call than quote a generic number.
Why bring on a director without a board track record?
Because the governance requirement and the judgment requirement aren't the same thing. The certification satisfies the first. Twenty-four years of P&L ownership, building functions from zero, and reading investor-facing numbers satisfies the second, and that doesn't require prior board tenure to be real.
What does an independent director actually do, day to day versus in board meetings?
The role runs on the company's meeting schedule, not daily involvement: board and committee meetings, with independent judgment on strategy, risk, and related-party matters brought to bear during and between those meetings, not day-to-day operational input.
