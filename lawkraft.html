A Few Honest Questions About Lawkraft

Hey Will, before I put any real thinking down on paper about how I can help Lawkraft, I'd rather ask you directly than guess. There are 24 quick questions below, mostly Yes, No or pick-one, a few with a short example so the question is clear. Nothing here takes any real writing from you, it should take about 15 minutes.

Answer them the way things actually are today, not the way you'd like them to be. The honest version is the only one that helps me see Lawkraft clearly enough to tell you something useful back.

One more thing: once you send this, you'll have 24 hours to come back to this same page and change any answer you want — your answers will already be sitting there, filled in. After that window, I'll treat what you've sent as final.

Section 1

Who You're Actually Selling To

1. Is there a one-page description of your ideal client, something like "bank or NBFC, 50+ active cases, cheque-bounce or post-judgment work", that everyone on your team uses to judge if a lead is worth chasing? Or does each person go by their own instinct?
Situation
2. A cooperative bank calls you with 200 overdue loan accounts, but they want everything handled over phone calls and physical courier, no digital uploads, no dashboard logins at all. What would you actually do?
3. Have you worked out, even roughly, how big the opportunity is in cooperative banks, housing finance companies, and Asset Reconstruction Companies specifically, the kinds of lenders that still run this process on spreadsheets and local lawyers?

Section 2

How Clients Actually Find And Pick You

4. Thinking about your current clients honestly, how did most of them really come in the door?
5. If I called three different people on your team and asked "why should a bank pick Lawkraft over the law firm they've used for ten years", would I get the same answer from all three?
Situation
6. A prospect tells you, "we're also getting a quote from Credgenics for the same work." Does your team have one consistent answer for why that comparison doesn't quite hold up, since Credgenics mostly works the stage before a case ever reaches court?
7. From a serious first conversation with a prospect to a signed deal, how long does it usually take?

Section 3

What A Prospect Sees Before Saying Yes

8. Beyond the two client quotes on your website, do you have even one case study with real numbers (cases closed, amount recovered, time taken) that a prospect can read on their own, without you in the room to explain it?
9. If a bank's risk or compliance team asked how you handle debtor data, cheque images, and court documents, and whether your advocates follow RBI's fair-practice recovery rules, could someone on your team answer that in writing today, without scrambling?
Situation
10. An NBFC's risk head asks for a sample of your monthly reporting before they'll sign anything. What's actually true today?

Section 4

Running The Business, And The Numbers That Matter For Growth

11. If you and your co-founders stepped away from new business development for a month, would new client conversations still be happening?
12. Once a client's first batch of 50 cases is running smoothly, is there a set next step to go after the rest of their portfolio, or does that depend on whether someone remembers to ask?
Situation
13. If you landed 3 new clients in the next 90 days, what would actually happen?
Situation
14. A rival drops its fee by 30% to win an account you're also pitching. What would you most likely do?
15. Roughly what share of your revenue today comes from your single biggest client?
16. How do you typically charge a client today?
17. Once a client signs, does new work keep flowing to you automatically as new cases come up, or does each new batch need a fresh conversation and approval?
18. Where does Lawkraft stand on capital today?

Section 5

How You See The Market

19. Outside large banks and NBFCs, which part of the lending market do you believe is most underserved today by any organized legal recovery provider, not software for soft collections, the actual on-the-ground legal execution?
20. Outside banks and NBFCs, has your team had a serious conversation with any other type of lender (a cooperative bank, an ARC, a housing finance company, a BNPL player) in the last year?
21. If you haven't pursued lender types outside banks and NBFCs so far, what's the main reason?
22. Outside your current clients, would someone running collections at a large Asset Reconstruction Company know that Lawkraft exists?
Situation
23. If a platform like Credgenics offered to hand you their cases once they reach court, for a cut of what you recover, would you take that deal? Or does working with a platform like that feel more like competing against it?
24. If you had to chase ONE new kind of lender hard over the next six months, which would it be?
