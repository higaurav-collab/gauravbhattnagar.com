Post-Acquisition GTM Integration

Merging two go-to-market motions cleanly, before customers notice the seams.

Gallup found acquired banks' customer attrition climbs to roughly 8% a year, against a 5% baseline. That gap is rarely about the deal. It's about what happens after.

Most of the attention in a merger goes to the deal itself: price, structure, legal terms. Almost none of it goes to the morning after, when two sales teams, two brands, and two sets of customer-facing systems suddenly have to work as one.

That gap isn't about the acquisition being a bad idea. It's about go-to-market integration nobody planned for properly.

This service is that planning: merging two go-to-market motions, brands, and sales organisations cleanly, after a deal has already closed.

How This Works

Here's What This Actually Looks Like

  • Mapping where the two companies' sales processes, pricing, and customer-facing systems actually conflict, not just where the org charts overlap.
  • A sequencing plan for which parts of the GTM motion merge first, so customers and reps aren't left confused mid-transition.
  • Direct work with both sales and marketing teams to resolve brand, positioning, and messaging conflicts before customers spot them.
  • Tracking customer and revenue retention through the integration window, not just assuming it's fine.

The operator background here comes from building and running marketing functions from the ground up, at Prakhar Software Solutions and PCG DigiVerse, including hand-picking a 24-person team and launching it inside three months. Knowing how to build a GTM function from nothing is close to the same skill as knowing how to merge two that already exist without breaking either.

This starts once a deal has closed. Evaluating whether to do the deal at all, and what the target's GTM story is really worth, is the separate GTM Due Diligence for Investors service.

Good fit if:

a deal has closed and you need the two go-to-market motions actually merged, not just left to collide slowly.

Not a fit if:

the deal hasn't closed yet and you're still deciding whether to do it. That's GTM Due Diligence for Investors instead.

Frequently Asked

FAQs: Post-Acquisition GTM Integration

When should this start?
As close to deal close as possible. The longer two GTM motions run uncoordinated, the more customer confusion and attrition sets in.
What's the biggest risk you're watching for?
Customers and reps caught in the middle: conflicting pricing, confused positioning, or two systems that don't talk to each other.
How long does an integration typically take?
It depends on how different the two motions were going in, but most run several months, sequenced in stages rather than a single cutover.
Do you work with both companies' teams?
Yes, directly with sales and marketing on both sides, since the conflicts usually show up at the point where the two teams actually have to work together.
How do you know if it's working?
Customer and revenue retention tracked through the integration window, not assumed. If something's slipping, it shows up early enough to fix.
How is this different from GTM Due Diligence for Investors?
That runs before the deal closes, checking whether the target's growth story is real. This runs after, actually merging the two GTM motions.
