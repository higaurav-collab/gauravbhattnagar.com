One in Three Companies Has No CMO Right Now. Is Yours Next?

Somewhere in your leadership team right now, there may be a gap nobody has named out loud. Not a resignation. Not a reorg announcement. Just a marketing function that used to report to a CMO, and now reports… somewhere else. To the CEO directly. To a CRO. To whoever inherited it when the last person left and the seat was never actually refilled.

If that sounds oddly specific, it is because it is happening at a scale most leadership teams have not clocked yet. It is not a signal that marketing stopped mattering. It is a signal that the job of proving marketing matters got harder, and a lot of companies would rather quietly restructure around that problem than solve it.

The Global Signal

At large, mature companies, the CMO seat itself is quietly disappearing.

Roughly a third of S&P 500 companies now have no designated enterprise CMO. The responsibility has moved to a Chief Revenue Officer, a Chief Commercial Officer, or regional leaders instead.

What it means

This isn't marketing losing relevance. It's the seat losing its blank cheque. Companies at this scale still need senior marketing judgment. They have just stopped being willing to fund four years of it before anyone can prove it worked.

The India Signal

Here, the pressure shows up differently: not the seat disappearing, but nobody staying in it long enough to build what the board wants.

In India, the seat itself is not going away. The problem is churn: CMOs are leaving faster than the measurement and reporting muscle the board is now demanding can get built.

What it means

Indian companies are not choosing between a CMO and no CMO. They are choosing between hiring the wrong senior leader permanently, or building the function properly with someone who has done it before, while the business is still working out what the role should even own.

Why This Is a Leadership Model Problem, Not a Hiring Problem

Read both signals as a hiring brief and they point to the same underlying shift, arrived at from different directions. Companies still need senior marketing judgment. What has changed is how much runway they are willing to give someone to prove it is working. Fractional and interim leadership is not a cheaper CMO. It is the same accountability, priced and timed to match how much certainty the business actually has right now.

FAQ

Fractional CMOs, plainly explained

How is a fractional CMO different from a marketing consultant or agency?
A consultant advises from outside. An agency executes campaigns. A fractional CMO sits inside the leadership team and is accountable for the marketing function's outcomes the same way a full time CMO would be, just for a defined number of days or hours a week rather than five days permanently.
How many hours or days a week does a fractional CMO actually work?
Most engagements run ten to twenty hours a week, though the split depends on company stage and what needs building first. What matters more than hours is scope. A fractional CMO should hold real decision rights over strategy, budget, and team, not just a seat in the room.
Can a fractional CMO manage an existing marketing team, or do they only advise?
They manage. A fractional CMO typically becomes the direct manager of the marketing team for the engagement, owning hiring, performance, and vendor decisions the same way a full time CMO would.
What happens when the engagement ends?
A well run engagement has an exit built in from day one. That might be a documented handoff to an internal hire, a transition to a full time search once the business has grown into that need, or an extension if the mandate has genuinely expanded. The outcome to avoid is an engagement that quietly continues with no criteria for what comes next.
