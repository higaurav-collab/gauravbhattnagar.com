Guest Faculty / Coaching

Get real-world marketing practice brought into your classroom or program.

Most guest lectures on marketing come from someone who read the case study. This one comes from someone who ran the budget.

I taught a digital marketing cohort to MBA students at a leading business school across the 2018-19 and 2019-20 batches. What I brought wasn't a textbook framework. It was the same budget-and-accountability lens I was using with real clients at the time, translated into a classroom.

Most guest sessions default to case studies from ten years ago or frameworks with no P&L attached. I teach from what I'm actually doing: how a campaign gets budgeted, what happens when it underperforms, how you tell a client the number didn't move and what you do next.

Frequently Asked

FAQs: Guest Faculty / Coaching

What topics do you teach or coach on?
Practical digital marketing and GTM: campaign budgeting, channel strategy, measuring what actually returns revenue, and how to course-correct when a campaign underperforms, the same decisions I make with real clients.
Can you name the institution you've taught at?
I keep that unnamed by choice, for reasons unrelated to the quality of the work. What I can speak to in detail is the cohort, the curriculum, and the outcomes.
What format do you teach in: a single session or a full course?
Both, depending on what the program needs. I've run single guest sessions and full-cohort engagements across two academic years.
Is this only for MBA programs?
No. MBA cohorts are where I've taught so far, but the same session format works for executive education, corporate training programs, and industry workshops.
How is your teaching different from a typical guest lecture?
Most guest sessions default to case studies from a decade ago or frameworks with no P&L attached. I teach from what I'm actually doing right now: real budgets, real numbers, real mistakes.
Do you charge for guest sessions?
It depends on the format and the institution. Happy to discuss on a call rather than quote a flat rate that doesn't fit every setting.
