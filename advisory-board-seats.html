Advisory Board Seats

Get senior marketing judgment at your leadership table, without adding headcount.

Most boards get a generalist opinion on marketing. Yours can get someone who has actually owned the budget and the number it produced.

I'll say this plainly: I'm opening this up now, not pointing to a roster of boards I already sit on. What I bring instead is 24 years split across being the operator inside a corporate account, the founder who built and sold a creative business, and the outside advisor who has already done the mentorship version of this, well before any formal seat or fee was involved.

How This Works

Here's What This Actually Looks Like

  • A monthly meeting with the board, built around the two or three decisions that actually move the business.
  • Direct access in between meetings, for decisions that can't wait.
  • A narrow, specific mandate: marketing and GTM judgment, not a generalist opinion on everything the business does.
  • Judgment built running real budgets and teams, not theoretical frameworks.

An advisory seat, done properly, means showing up with a specific point of view on the decisions that matter, not general encouragement once a month. That's the judgment I've built running budgets, building teams from zero, and being accountable for what a marketing function returns.

Good fit if:

you want a specific, marketing-and-GTM point of view at the table as you scale, and you'd rather bring someone in now, early in their board-advisory track, than wait.

Not a fit if:

you need someone with an existing board track record for governance or investor-optics reasons. I'd rather tell you that upfront than take the seat anyway.

Frequently Asked

FAQs: Advisory Board Seats

What does an advisory board seat with you actually involve?
A monthly meeting focused on a narrow mandate, the two or three marketing and GTM decisions that actually move the business, plus direct access in between when something can't wait.
Why should I consider someone without prior board experience?
Because the judgment a board seat needs, reading a number, owning a budget, knowing when a plan isn't working, doesn't require a board title to build. I've built that judgment as an operator, a founder, and an advisor. The seat is the credential I'm building now, not one I'm claiming early.
What's the typical time commitment?
A monthly meeting is standard, with direct access in between for decisions that can't wait. It's built to be high-leverage, not a second full-time role.
How is this different from hiring you fractionally?
Fractional leadership means I sit inside your business and own an ongoing number directly. An advisory seat means a narrower, higher-level mandate: judgment on specific decisions, not day-to-day execution.
What stage or size of company is this best suited for?
Businesses scaling past the point where founder instinct alone is enough, but not yet ready for, or needing, a full board with formal governance requirements.
How do you decide whether to take a seat?
Whether I have a specific, useful point of view on the two or three decisions the business is actually facing. If I don't, I'd rather say so than take the seat anyway.
